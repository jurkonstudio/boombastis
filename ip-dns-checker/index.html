About this tool

This tool shows your public IP address and looks up DNS records for any domain. It is useful for checking what a website sees when you connect, confirming that a VPN is working, and verifying DNS changes after you move a site or edit records.

How it works

Your public IP is detected with a request to ipify, and the country and ISP come from ipwho.is. DNS records are fetched with DNS over HTTPS straight from Cloudflare (1.1.1.1) and Google (8.8.8.8) and shown side by side, so you can see whether two major resolvers agree.

How to use

  1. Open the page to see your public IP, country and ISP.
  2. Type a domain and press Look up.
  3. Compare the Cloudflare and Google answers for each record type.

Frequently asked questions

What is my public IP address?

It is the address the internet sees when you connect, assigned by your internet provider or by your VPN. It is different from the private address your router gives your device, such as 192.168.1.5.

Why does my IP show a different city or country?

IP location data is an estimate based on where the provider registered the address. It is often accurate at country level and unreliable at city level, and it shows the VPN server’s location if you use a VPN.

What do the DNS record types mean?

A and AAAA map a name to an IPv4 or IPv6 address. CNAME points a name to another name. MX lists mail servers, NS lists the servers that answer for the domain, and TXT holds text such as SPF and verification codes.

Why do Cloudflare and Google sometimes return different answers?

Large sites use CDNs that return different addresses by region or load, and a recent DNS change may not have reached every resolver yet. A difference is not necessarily a problem.

How long do DNS changes take to show up?

Up to the record’s TTL, which can range from a few minutes to a day or more. Lower the TTL before a planned change if you need it to spread quickly.

Does this tool store my IP or searches?

Boombastis does not log or store them. Your browser contacts the lookup providers directly, and those providers see the request like any other website you visit.
