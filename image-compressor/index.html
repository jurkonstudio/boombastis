Image Compressor

Shrink and convert images right in your browser.

About this tool

Large photos slow down websites, fill up email attachments and eat storage. This image compressor shrinks JPG, PNG and WebP files and converts between formats, and it does everything on your own device. Pick a format, a maximum width and a quality level, and you get an instant before-and-after size for every file.

How it works

Each image is decoded by your browser, resized if it is wider than the limit you chose, and encoded again in the selected format. Quality 70 to 80 is a good starting point for photos: the file gets much smaller and the difference is hard to see. Lower values save more space but add visible artifacts.

How to use

  1. Drop or choose one or more images.
  2. Pick WebP, JPEG or PNG, then a maximum width and quality.
  3. Download each result, or check the total size you saved.

Frequently asked questions

Are my images uploaded anywhere?

No. Compression runs inside your browser, so your photos never leave your device and nothing is stored on a server.

Which format should I choose?

WebP usually gives the smallest file at similar visual quality and is supported by all modern browsers. Choose JPEG when you need the widest compatibility, for example for old software or some email clients. Choose PNG only when you need lossless output or transparency.

Why did my transparent PNG get a white background?

JPEG does not support transparency, so transparent areas are filled with white when you export to JPEG. Pick WebP or PNG to keep transparency.

Does compression remove photo metadata?

Yes. Re-encoding through the browser drops EXIF data such as camera model and GPS location. That is handy for privacy, but it also means the original capture details are not kept in the compressed copy.

What happens to animated GIFs?

Only the first frame is kept, because the image is redrawn on a canvas. Use a dedicated tool if you need to keep the animation.

Why is the result sometimes marked as already optimal?

If the re-encoded file would be larger than the original, the tool shows that the original is already well compressed. Try a lower quality or a smaller maximum width.
