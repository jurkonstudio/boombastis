    About this tool

    This CPS test measures how many times you can click in a set time. Pick 1, 5, 10, 30 or 60 seconds, click the large area as fast as you can, and the page shows your clicks per second (CPS), your total clicks and a rough level. It is a quick way to compare your clicking speed with friends, test a new mouse, or practice for games where fast clicking matters.

    How it works

    The timer starts on your first click, not when you press a button, so you never lose time getting ready. Every click counts until the time runs out, and clicks after that are ignored. Your CPS is the number of clicks divided by the length of the test. A 10 second test with 62 clicks gives 6.2 CPS.

    Only the left mouse button, a screen tap, or the Space and Enter keys count. Holding a key down does not add clicks. The test runs in your browser. If you choose to share a result, the leaderboard stores only the name you type, your score, the test length and the time of submission. No account or email is needed.

    How to use

    1. Choose a test length: 1, 5, 10, 30 or 60 seconds.
    2. Click or tap the large area as fast as you can. The timer starts with your first click.
    3. Read your result when time is up, and press Restart to try again.
    4. Optionally enter a name and submit your score to the leaderboard for that test length.

    What is a good CPS score?

    As a rough guide, under 4 CPS is casual clicking, 4 to 6 is average, 6 to 8 is quick, 8 to 10 is fast, and above 10 is very fast. Short tests give higher numbers than long ones, because you can sprint for one second but not for a minute. Your device also matters: mice, trackpads and touchscreens register clicks differently, so compare scores on the same device.

    Tips to click faster

    Keep your hand relaxed and your forearm supported. Click with small, quick finger movements instead of pressing hard, and keep the mouse still. Some players use jitter clicking or butterfly clicking, but these can strain your hand, so build up slowly. If you feel pain or numbness, stop and rest. Taking short breaks between tests also gives you more honest scores.

    Frequently asked questions

    What does CPS mean?

    CPS stands for clicks per second. It is the number of times you click in a given time divided by the number of seconds. For example, 50 clicks in 10 seconds is 5 CPS.

    What is a good CPS score?

    Most people land between about 4 and 7 CPS on a 5 or 10 second test. Above 8 CPS is quick, and above 10 CPS is very fast and usually takes practice or a special clicking technique. Treat these numbers as a rough guide, because your mouse, trackpad or screen affects the result.

    Why is my score lower on longer tests?

    Short tests measure a burst of speed. In a 30 or 60 second test your hand and forearm tire, so your average CPS drops. This is normal, which is why the leaderboard is kept separate for each test length.

    How can I click faster?

    Rest your forearm, keep your hand relaxed and click with small, quick movements instead of pressing hard. Some people use jitter clicking (tensing the arm so the finger vibrates) or butterfly clicking (alternating two fingers on one button). Stop if your hand or wrist hurts, and take breaks between tests.

    Does the test work on a phone or tablet?

    Yes. Tap the click area with one finger or alternate fingers. Scores on a touchscreen are often different from a mouse, so compare your results on the same type of device.

    Can I use the keyboard?

    Yes. Focus the click area with Tab and press Space or Enter. Holding the key down does not count extra clicks.

    Is my score saved?

    Only if you press Submit score. Then your chosen name, score, test length and the time are stored in the public leaderboard. Without that, only your best score for each test length is remembered in your own browser.

    Can people cheat on the leaderboard?

    The test runs in the browser, so a determined person can fake a score. We apply basic limits, but the leaderboard is meant for fun, not for competitions with prizes.
