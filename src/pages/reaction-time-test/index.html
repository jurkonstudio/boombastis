      About this tool

      This reaction time test shows how quickly you respond to something you see. The area turns red while you wait, then green at a random moment. Click as soon as it turns green. After five rounds you get your average reaction time in milliseconds, your fastest round and a rough level. It is a simple way to compare yourself with friends or check how alert you feel.

      How it works

      When you start a round, the area turns red and the page waits a random time, between about 1.5 and 4.5 seconds. Then it turns green and a high-resolution timer starts. The moment you click, tap or press Space or Enter, the timer stops and the page shows the result in milliseconds. If you click while it is still red, the round does not count and you try it again.

      Your score is the average of five rounds. The test runs in your browser. If you choose to share a result, the leaderboard stores only the name you type, your average time and the time of submission. No account or email is needed.

      How to use

      1. Press the colored area to start the first round.
      2. Wait for it to turn green. Do not click early.
      3. Click as fast as you can when it turns green.
      4. Repeat for five rounds, then read your average and fastest time.
      5. Optionally enter a name and submit your average to the leaderboard.

      What is a good reaction time?

      Most people score around 200 to 300 milliseconds on an online visual test. Under 200 ms is excellent, 200 to 250 ms is good, 250 to 300 ms is average, and above 400 ms is slow. These are rough ranges: a fast monitor and a wired mouse can lower your times by a few tens of milliseconds, while a touchscreen or a slow display can add delay. For a fair comparison, always test on the same setup.

      Tips for faster reactions

      Sit comfortably, rest your hand on the mouse and look at the center of the colored area. Do not guess when green will come, because early clicks only waste time. Play when you are rested and focused, because tiredness slows reactions. Close other busy tabs so your browser is not slowed down. This test is for fun and practice and is not a medical measurement.

      Frequently asked questions

      How is reaction time measured?

      The test measures the time between the moment the screen turns green and the moment you click or press a key. It uses your browser's high-resolution timer and reports milliseconds. You play five rounds and the average is your score.

      What is a good reaction time?

      Most people score around 200 to 300 milliseconds on an online visual reaction test. Under 200 ms is excellent. Results depend on your screen, mouse or touchscreen, and how alert you are, so compare scores on the same device.

      What happens if I click too soon?

      The screen turns blue, that round does not count, and you click to try it again. The wait before green is random, between about 1.5 and 4.5 seconds, so you cannot predict it.

      Why do my results change from test to test?

      Human reaction time varies from one attempt to the next. Tiredness, distraction, caffeine, the device you use and even how closely you watch the screen all play a part. The five-round average smooths out single lucky or slow rounds.

      Is the test accurate to the millisecond?

      No. Your screen refresh rate, input device and browser add small delays that the page cannot see, typically a few tens of milliseconds. The numbers are good for comparing yourself over time on the same setup, not for scientific measurement.

      Is this a medical test?

      No. This is a simple game for fun and practice. If you are worried about your reflexes or health, talk to a doctor.

      Can I use the keyboard?

      Yes. Focus the colored area with Tab and press Space or Enter instead of clicking.

      Is my score saved?

      Only if you press Submit score. Then your chosen name, average time and the time of submission are stored in the public leaderboard. Without that, only your best average is remembered in your own browser.
